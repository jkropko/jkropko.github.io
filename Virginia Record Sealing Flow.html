Virginia Record Sealing Flow

The decision tree a guided web form follows to classify one record from a person's court file, plus the inputs it collects and the report it returns. Built from §§ 19.2-392.5 through 19.2-392.17 as published October 2026.

Five outcomes

Automatic · nowSealed by law or at the next sweep
Automatic · laterSealed on a computed date if the record stays clean
Petition · nowEligible to file in circuit court today
Petition · laterEligible to file on a computed date
Not eligiblePermanently, or until a stated condition changes

A sixth state, Not yet determinable, covers pending cases. Each outcome box in the diagrams shows the statute it rests on.

Date formulas

RuleEligibility dateClean-record conditionSource
TrafficFinal disposition + 11 yearsNone§ 19.2-392.17
Annual sweepLatest Virginia arrest or charge + 3 years (next annual review)No unsealed Virginia conviction, ever§ 19.2-392.11
Monthly sweepConviction date + 7 years (next monthly review)No reportable conviction anywhere in those 7 years§ 19.2-392.6(B)
Minor-offense petitionConviction or dismissal date + 7 yearsSame as monthly sweep§ 19.2-392.12:1(I)
General petitionLatest of: S + 7 years (misdemeanor) or + 10 years (felony); last Class 3–4 felony conviction + 20 years; last other felony conviction + 10 yearsNo reportable conviction anywhere from S through S + 7/10 years§ 19.2-392.12(F)(1)–(2)

S is the latest of: conviction or dismissal of the deferred charge; release from incarceration on this case; a finding of a probation, parole or suspended-sentence violation on this case; release after that violation. "Reportable" means an offense reportable to the Central Criminal Records Exchange under § 19.2-390(A), in Virginia, another state, D.C., or federal or territorial courts.

What the report tells the user

Automatic · now

Says
This record is sealed by law, or will be at the next monthly or annual State Police review.
Next step
None. If it still shows up later, a free petition under § 19.2-392.12:1 is available for minor-offense records.

Automatic · later

Says
Sealing happens on or after [date] if no new disqualifying record appears.
Warns
Which events would change this: a new conviction, or a new arrest or charge for annual-sweep cases.

Petition · now

Says
You can file today in the circuit court where the case ended. No filing fee.
Lists
Petition contents, serving the prosecutor (30-day response window), and requesting your record from the Central Criminal Records Exchange. For § 19.2-392.12: manifest-injustice showing, rehabilitation if substance-related, and that it uses one of two lifetime grants.

Petition · later

Says
You can file on or after [date], once [restitution is paid].
Shows
Which input set the date (clean period, felony lookback, or restitution) so the user can see what to wait for.

Not eligible

Says
The specific reason and the statute.
Marks
Permanent versus "until" (unsealed convictions, gun rights, animal ban), and points to expungement under § 19.2-392.2 for non-conviction outcomes.

Every report ends with

Effects
You may deny a sealed record to most employers and agencies, except police jobs, jobs where state or federal law requires asking, and security clearances. Courts may still use it at sentencing, bail and custody hearings. Sealing doesn't restore gun or civil rights, or cancel fines and restitution (§ 19.2-392.5).
