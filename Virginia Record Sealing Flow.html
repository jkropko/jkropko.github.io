Virginia Record Sealing Flow

The decision tree a guided web form follows to classify one record from a person's court file, plus the inputs it collects and the report it returns. Built from §§ 19.2-392.5 through 19.2-392.17 as published October 2026, and revised October 30, 2026 against Virginia State Crime Commission and Virginia Indigent Defense Commission materials (see “What changed” below).

Five outcomes

Automatic · nowSealed by law or at the next sweep
Automatic · laterSealed on a computed date if the record stays clean
Petition · nowEligible to file in circuit court today
Petition · laterEligible to file on a computed date
Not eligiblePermanently, or until a stated condition changes

A sixth state, Not yet determinable, covers pending cases. Each outcome box in the diagrams shows the statute it rests on.

§ 19.2-392.8 applies only to cases that end on or after July 1, 2026. Felony non-convictions from before then have no sealing process. Every non-conviction outcome also tells the user about expungement, which the Virginia Indigent Defense Commission recommends because it gives more privacy than sealing. The title of § 19.2-392.11 mentions deferred dismissals, but its text bars only convictions, so the form flags that case as uncertain.

Date formulas

RuleEligibility dateClean-record conditionSource
TrafficFinal disposition + 11 yearsNone§ 19.2-392.17
Annual sweepLater of final disposition + 3 years and latest reportable Virginia arrest or charge + 3 years (next annual review)No unsealed reportable Virginia conviction, ever§ 19.2-392.11
Monthly sweepConviction date + 7 years (next monthly review)No reportable conviction anywhere in those 7 years§ 19.2-392.6(B)
Minor-offense petitionConviction or dismissal date + 7 yearsSame as monthly sweep§ 19.2-392.12:1(I)
General petitionLatest of: S + 7 years (misdemeanor) or + 10 years (felony); most recent reportable conviction after S + 7 / 10 years; last Class 3–4 felony conviction + 20 years; last other felony conviction + 10 yearsA full 7 / 10 years with no reportable conviction; a new conviction restarts the clock (VIDC reading)§ 19.2-392.12(F)(1)–(2)

S is the latest of: conviction or dismissal of the deferred charge; release from incarceration on this case; a finding of a probation, parole or suspended-sentence violation on this case; release after that violation. "Reportable" means reportable to the Central Criminal Records Exchange under § 19.2-390(A): mainly felonies and jailable misdemeanors. Fine-only Class 3 and 4 misdemeanors are not reported, and most Title 46.2 offenses such as reckless driving are not either, so they do not restart or block a waiting period. Convictions in another state, D.C., or federal or territorial courts count unless they are traffic infractions. Nothing could be sealed before July 1, 2026, so the form shows "eligible since" as July 1, 2026 for older records.

What the report tells the user

Automatic · now

Says
This record is sealed by law, or will be at the next monthly or annual State Police review.
Next step
None. People aren't notified, so the report explains how to check (OCIS, the clerk, or a State Police record, which marks sealed offenses), and that automatic sealing only works if the offense reached the CCRE. If it doesn't seal, a free petition under § 19.2-392.12:1 is the backup. Non-conviction results also mention expungement.

Automatic · later

Says
Sealing happens on or after [date] if no new disqualifying record appears.
Warns
Which events would change this: a new conviction, or a new arrest or charge for annual-sweep cases.

Petition · now

Says
You can file today in the circuit court where the case ended. No filing fee.
Lists
The right forms (CC-1201 and CC-1201(A) for the general petition; CC-1203 and its addenda for the automatic petition; cover sheet CC-1416), copies of the charging documents, filing with the circuit court civil division, serving the prosecutor (30 days), and requesting the criminal history with State Police form SP-356 (2–4 weeks). For § 19.2-392.12: manifest-injustice showing, rehabilitation if substance-related, and that it uses one of two lifetime grants.

Petition · later

Says
You can file on or after [date], once [restitution is paid].
Shows
Which input set the date (clean period, a newer conviction, felony lookback, or restitution) so the user can see what to wait for. A newer conviction adds a note that this follows the VIDC reading.

Not eligible

Says
The specific reason and the statute.
Marks
Permanent versus "until" (unsealed convictions, gun rights, animal ban), and points to expungement under § 19.2-392.2 for non-conviction outcomes.

Every report ends with

Effects
You may deny a sealed record to most Virginia employers and agencies, except police jobs, jobs where law requires asking, security clearances, jury service and custody cases. Federal (FBI) records aren't affected, and federal firearm, housing and barrier-crime rules still apply. Courts may still use it at sentencing and bail. Background check companies must delete it. Sealing isn't a pardon and doesn't restore gun or civil rights, or cancel fines and restitution (§ 19.2-392.5).
